Setup

DataThe violin takes of MOSA (Huang et al., 2024) with their aligned scores, double stops removed: 14 pieces, 15 players, 207,172 notes.
SplitBy piece: training 13.4 h, validation 1.2 h (be8), test 2.3 h (de2, mo4).
CurvesPitch, loudness and brightness α, 250 frames per second, read from the recording.
NotesPitch, onset, end, velocity, slurred or new bow, brightness. From the aligned score and the curves.
Expression controlsPer note: attack time, crescendo, glide time, vibrato rate, depth and delay. Measured on the note's curves.
SynthesiserDDSP-Violin from curves + room reverb, 100K steps (Part 2), one per training set.
Audio20 s per passage, level matched to the recording.
LossSpectral loss to the recording through the all-players synthesiser, median over the 45 validation and test files. Vibrato out of time with the recording counts as error.

Models

Control model

  • Network: a bidirectional GRU over the notes, then an autoregressive GRU note by note, as the expression generator of MIDI-DDSP (Wu et al., ICLR 2022). One mixture of Gaussians per control, sampled.
  • Training: the likelihood of the measured controls it is not shown, 10,000 steps.

Curve model

  • Output: each curve as its movement around the note's value.
  • Network: a bidirectional GRU over the notes gives one vector per note. Dilated convolutions over the frames (±0.5 s) write the curves.
  • Training: L1 on the three curves, each divided by its spread, 30,000 steps.

Part 2: a discriminator on the curves

Does a discriminator bring back the movement that differs in every take?

Planned, after the synthesiser's own discriminator (Part 4).

  • Why
    • The L1 loss rewards the average of what the notes do not settle, so the written curves are smoother than a take's.
    • MIDI-DDSP (Wu et al., ICLR 2022) reports the same of its generator and adds a discriminator on its output for it.
  • Discriminator
    • Reads the three curves, measured or written, with the notes they belong to.
    • Network, as MIDI-DDSP's: 1-D convolutions over the frames, at three time scales.
    • What the curve model gets, beside the L1 loss: the adversarial loss and feature matching.
  • Before it
    • The curve model continues through the frozen synthesiser, on the spectral loss to the recording.
