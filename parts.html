Setup

QuestionWhich part of the sound makes a model sound synthetic: its harmonics, or what lies beside them?
ExcerptsThe long-note excerpt of each dataset's training and test sets, as on the synthesiser's page.
Harmonics aloneRecordings: the sinusoids taken by sinusoidal modelling, those of earlier notes still sounding included. Models: the harmonics of the output at the multiples of the pitch.
Noise targetThe recording without its harmonics, without the other notes and without short bursts. The noise loss trains the noise on it.
Noise target from the generatorThe synthesiser's noise (filtered noise, 65 bands) set to the target's band levels every 4 ms.
Harmonics + generated noiseWhat a model taught this noise could sound like.
ModelsTrained on one MOSA player, 50K steps, one seed. Each output at the recording's level, split as the recording in the table's first row: the harmonics at the multiples of the pitch, and the noise part, which is the output minus them.
Part 4 modelDDSP-Violin from curves with the minimum-phase body, a room reverb of 1 s and the residual range at ±12 dB.
Noise trained on the noise targetThe noise has its own loss against that target, and the spectral loss no longer trains it.
Noise of two numbers per frameA level against the harmonics and a tilt, on one learnt curve over the bands, pulsed once per pitch period.
Harmonic lossThe levels of the 40 harmonics of the output against the recording's.
Test lossSpectral loss on the test windows, as on the synthesiser's page.
Noise part, dBIts level against the whole, inside held notes | at attacks and note changes.
