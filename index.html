Part 1: the original DDSP against DDSP-Violin

Do the physical constraints cost anything when both models read the audio they resynthesise?

Test loss: without z, DDSP-Violin + room reverb is level with DDSP on Garner (6.97 against 6.94) and on ev03 (5.89 both) and 0.39 below it on all players (5.93 against 6.32). With z, it is below DDSP + z on every set: by 0.18 on Garner, 0.04 on ev03 and 0.29 on all players.

DDSP

The model of Engel et al. (ICLR 2020), run with the published code (magenta/ddsp 3.7.0, solo_instrument.gin).

  • Inputs: pitch and loudness. DDSP + z adds the z encoder of the paper.
  • Decoder outputs per frame: amplitude, 60 harmonic amplitudes, 65 noise-band magnitudes.
  • Reverb: trainable, 3 s (48,000 samples), on the sum of harmonics and noise, dry signal added. No body filter.
Diagram

From Engel et al. (2020).

DDSP-Violin

The model of our AES 2026 paper (code).

  • Inputs: pitch, loudness and the harmonic amplitudes of the audio, read by the harmonic encoder. + z adds the main z encoder of the paper.
  • From the harmonic encoder: the brightness α and the bow position k, fed to the decoder and to the masks.
  • Decoder outputs per frame: amplitude, 60 per-harmonic residuals (within ±6 dB), 65 noise-band magnitudes.
  • Body filter: learned, 2,048 samples, on the harmonic part.
  • Residual loss at weight 0.3.
  • + room reverb: the same trainable 3 s reverb as DDSP, on the sum of the filtered harmonics and the noise.
Diagram
  • DDSP has its 3 s reverb: compare it with the DDSP-Violin + room reverb rows.
  • DDSP's loss curve on all players is jagged: its reader shuffles 1,000 examples at a time, 2% of that set.

Part 2: synthesis from curves

What is lost when the model reads nothing from the audio?

Test loss from curves + room reverb: 0.4 to 0.5 above the model that reads the audio (DDSP-Violin + z + room reverb) on every set. Against DDSP, equal on Garner (6.94), 0.19 above on ev03 (6.08 against 5.89) and 0.40 below on all players (5.92 against 6.32). The room reverb takes 0.02 to 0.18 off.

DDSP-Violin from curves

The synthesiser of the MIDI studies: DDSP-Violin without its encoders.

  • Inputs: pitch, loudness and the brightness α. Measured on the recording in training, given by the performance model at synthesis.
  • k: not predicted, the bow mask stays at the open position.
  • Decoder outputs, body filter, residual loss and + room reverb as in DDSP-Violin.

Part 3: one change at a time on the curves model

Which parts of the model limit it?

Test loss: two changes lower it clearly. One body filter per player: 5.79 against 5.92 (all players). The residual range at ±12 dB: 5.84 against 5.92 (all players) and 5.98 against 6.08 (ev03). The other changes stay within 0.05 of the base, or raise the loss because they train for something else as well (level loss, short windows, parameter loss). Garner's changes are still training.

Base: the curves model with its 3 s room reverb. The two parameter-loss rows and the notes row have one seed.

What each change does
  • Decoder inputs
    • The notes added: per frame, the place in the written note, whether the note starts with a new bow, and the note's attack, crescendo, vibrato and glide (all players only).
  • Harmonic source
    • Residual loss weight 0: the per-harmonic residuals free of their penalty.
    • Residual loss weight 1: the residuals held close to the physical mask.
    • Residual range ±12 dB: each harmonic may move twice as far from the mask.
  • Body filter
    • Minimum phase: the same magnitude response, no pre-ringing.
    • One per player: the player given as an input (all players only).
  • Room reverb
    • 0.5 s: shorter than the rooms' decay.
  • Noise
    • 257 bands: a finer noise spectrum.
  • Loss
    • Level loss: the output's loudness curve held to the recording's.
    • Parameter loss: the harmonic levels and noise bands of the output audio held to the recording's, both read the same way.
    • Short windows only: 2048 and 1024 dropped.

Part 4: the best changes in one model, with and without a discriminator

Do the gains of Part 3 add up, and does a discriminator bring the output closer to the recording?

Test loss: 6.64 with the discriminator, against 6.50 for the base and 6.54 continued without it. Parameter loss on the test windows (times 300): 7.78 against 7.36 and 7.44.

  • Combined model: planned.
  • Discriminator: a first trial on the one-player curves model, one seed. Both continued rows start from the base's 50K checkpoint. With the discriminator only the decoder trains, 40K steps, adversarial loss at weight 0.1 and feature matching at 1.0.
  • Combined model
    • The changes to keep are chosen after listening to Part 3.
  • Discriminator
    • A second network, used in training only. It reads 4 s of audio and learns to score recordings high and the model's outputs low (hinge loss).
    • Network: the multi-scale STFT discriminator of EnCodec (Défossez et al., 2022), with three scales instead of five. Per scale, a spectrogram (windows of 2048, 1024 and 512 samples, hop a quarter, real and imaginary parts) goes through five 2-D convolution layers of 32 channels (3 frames by 9 bins, three of them halving the frequency axis, dilations 1, 2 and 4 in time), then one layer that scores each patch of time and frequency.
    • What the decoder gets, beside the spectral loss, as in EnCodec and MelGAN (Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2019):
      • Adversarial loss: the scores of its output pushed up.
      • Feature matching: each layer's response to the output held to its response to the recording.
    • Training order: the combined model first, without it. Then the decoder continues with it, the rest of the model fixed.
    • MIDI-DDSP (Wu et al., ICLR 2022) has a discriminator on the synthesis parameters. Here it reads the audio.
