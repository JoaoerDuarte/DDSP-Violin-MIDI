DDSP-Violin MIDI: the synthesiser

Data, preprocessing and training shared by every part first, then one part per question with its models, audio and losses.

Datasets

The solo violin set is all training, as in the DDSP paper. The MOSA sets have a training set, a validation set and a test set of whole takes (training 13.8 h, of which 36 min of ev03).

Preprocessing

  • Audio: 16 kHz mono, one gain per dataset.
  • Frame rate: 250 per second (4 ms).
  • Pitch: CREPE (full model, Viterbi decoding). A frame without a tracked pitch takes the pitch of the nearest tracked frame.
  • Loudness: A-weighted power over 512-sample windows, in dB.
  • Harmonic amplitudes (DDSP-Violin only): the first 40 harmonics at the pitch, per frame.
  • Training examples: 4 s windows cut every second.

Training

  • Loss: multi-scale spectral, L1 on the magnitude and on the log magnitude, FFT sizes 2048, 1024, 512, 256, 128, 64.
  • Optimiser: Adam, 3e-4 decaying by 0.98 every 10K steps, gradient clip 3.
  • Batch 32, 50K steps, one model per dataset, two seeds.

How results are reported

  • Audio: the first 10 s of each excerpt, level matched to the recording, from the better of the two seeds. training set and test set say where the excerpt comes from.
  • Loss: the training loss, one implementation for every model, of the output against the recording on fixed windows, averaged over the seeds: the validation takes of the MOSA sets, which no model trained on; the training recordings of the solo violin set, which has no validation split.
  • One table per training set, models as rows. A pending cell is a model still in training or a score still to come.

Part 1: the original DDSP against DDSP-Violin

Do the physical constraints cost anything when both models read the audio they resynthesise?

DDSP

The model of Engel et al. (ICLR 2020), run with the published code (magenta/ddsp 3.7.0, solo_instrument.gin).

  • Inputs: pitch and loudness. DDSP + z adds the z encoder (MFCC, GRU 512, 16 values at 125 frames per 4 s).
  • Decoder: one 3-layer MLP (512) per input, GRU (512), 3-layer MLP (512).
  • Outputs per frame: amplitude, 60 harmonic amplitudes, 65 noise-band magnitudes.
  • Synthesis: harmonic oscillator bank plus filtered noise.
  • Reverb: trainable, 3 s (48,000 samples), on the sum of harmonics and noise, dry signal added. No body filter.
Diagram

From Engel et al. (2020).

DDSP-Violin

The model of our AES 2026 paper (code).

  • Inputs: pitch, loudness and the harmonic amplitudes of the audio, read by the harmonic encoder into z₂. + z adds the main z encoder as in the paper.
  • From z₂: the brightness α and the bow position k, fed to the decoder and to the masks.
  • Decoder: as DDSP.
  • Outputs per frame: amplitude, 60 per-harmonic residuals (within ±6 dB), 65 noise-band magnitudes.
  • Body filter: learned, 2,048 samples, on the harmonic part.
  • Residual loss at weight 0.3.
  • + room reverb: the same trainable 3 s reverb as DDSP, on the sum of the filtered harmonics and the noise.
Diagram

DDSP always carries its 3 s reverb; the DDSP-Violin + room reverb rows add the same one, so those are the like-for-like pairs. Longer training: the same settings continued, 150K steps on ev03 and 300K on all players, one seed.

Part 2: synthesis from curves

What is lost when the model reads nothing from the audio, only pitch, loudness and the measured brightness, as at synthesis from a score?

DDSP-Violin from curves

The synthesiser of the MIDI studies: the same model driven by curves alone, nothing read from the audio at synthesis.

  • Inputs: pitch, loudness and the brightness α, measured per frame on the recording during training, and coming from the score or the performance model at synthesis. No encoder.
  • k: not predicted; the bow mask stays at the open position.
  • Decoder, outputs, body filter, residual loss and the + room reverb variant as above.
