Preprocessing

  • Audio: 16 kHz mono, one gain per dataset.
  • Frame rate: 250 per second (4 ms).
  • Pitch: CREPE (full model, Viterbi decoding). A frame without a tracked pitch takes the pitch of the nearest tracked frame.
  • Loudness: A-weighted power over 512-sample windows, in dB.
  • Harmonic amplitudes: the first 40 harmonics at the pitch, per frame (the harmonic encoder's input, and where the brightness is measured).
  • Training examples: 4 s windows cut every second.

Training

  • Loss: multi-scale spectral, L1 on the magnitude and on the log magnitude, FFT sizes 2048, 1024, 512, 256, 128, 64.
  • Optimiser: Adam, 3e-4 decaying by 0.98 every 10K steps, gradient clip 3.
  • Batch 32, two seeds, one model per dataset. Steps: 50K, continued to 100K (the Steps column of the tables).

Results

  • Audio: 10 s per excerpt, level matched to the recording, one training and one test excerpt per set, no double stops.
  • Loss: the spectral loss of training, on fixed training and test windows, seeds averaged.

Part 1: the original DDSP against DDSP-Violin

Do the physical constraints cost anything when both models read the audio they resynthesise?

DDSP

The model of Engel et al. (ICLR 2020), run with the published code (magenta/ddsp 3.7.0, solo_instrument.gin).

  • Inputs: pitch and loudness. DDSP + z adds the z encoder of the paper.
  • Decoder outputs per frame: amplitude, 60 harmonic amplitudes, 65 noise-band magnitudes.
  • Reverb: trainable, 3 s (48,000 samples), on the sum of harmonics and noise, dry signal added. No body filter.
Diagram

From Engel et al. (2020).

DDSP-Violin

The model of our AES 2026 paper (code).

  • Inputs: pitch, loudness and the harmonic amplitudes of the audio, read by the harmonic encoder. + z adds the main z encoder of the paper.
  • From the harmonic encoder: the brightness α and the bow position k, fed to the decoder and to the masks.
  • Decoder outputs per frame: amplitude, 60 per-harmonic residuals (within ±6 dB), 65 noise-band magnitudes.
  • Body filter: learned, 2,048 samples, on the harmonic part.
  • Residual loss at weight 0.3.
  • + room reverb: the same trainable 3 s reverb as DDSP, on the sum of the filtered harmonics and the noise.
Diagram

DDSP always has its 3 s reverb: its like-for-like pairs are the DDSP-Violin + room reverb rows.

Part 2: synthesis from curves

What is lost when the model reads nothing from the audio, only pitch, loudness and the measured brightness, as at synthesis from a score?

DDSP-Violin from curves

The synthesiser of the MIDI studies: the same model driven by curves alone, nothing read from the audio at synthesis.

  • Inputs: pitch, loudness and the brightness α, measured per frame on the recording during training, and coming from the score or the performance model at synthesis. No encoder.
  • k: not predicted, the bow mask stays at the open position.
  • Decoder outputs, body filter, residual loss and the + room reverb variant as above.

Part 3: one change at a time on the curves model

Which parts of the model limit it?

The base is the curves model with its 3 s room reverb (Part 2). Each model below changes one thing and trains the same way, on the solo violin set, ev03 and all players, two seeds. Results come here as they land.

  • Harmonic source
    • Residual loss weight 0 instead of 0.3: the per-harmonic residuals free of their penalty.
    • Residual loss weight 1 instead of 0.3: the residuals held close to the physical mask.
    • Residual range ±12 dB instead of ±6 dB: each harmonic may move twice as far from the mask.
  • Body filter
    • Minimum phase instead of a free 2,048-sample filter: the same magnitude response, no pre-ringing.
    • One filter per player instead of one for all, the player given as an input (all players only).
  • Room reverb
    • 0.5 s instead of 3 s: shorter than the rooms' decay.
  • Noise
    • 257 bands instead of 65: a finer noise spectrum.
    • A shorter window for the noise filter's impulse response (planned).
  • Loss
    • The level loss added, weight 0.9 instead of 0: the output's loudness curve held to the recording's (planned).
    • The parameter loss added, read from the decoder: the harmonic levels and noise bands held to the ones fitted to the recording (planned).
    • The parameter loss added, measured on the output audio instead of read from the decoder (planned).
    • The spectral loss with its four short windows only, 2048 and 1024 dropped (planned).
    • A mel term added to the spectral loss (planned).
