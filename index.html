DDSP-Violin MIDI — Audio Examples

The original DDSP model and DDSP-Violin, trained with the same recipe on the same recordings.

This page compares two synthesisers that turn a pitch curve and a loudness curve into violin sound: the DDSP solo-instrument model of Engel et al. (ICLR 2020), run with its published code and recipe, and DDSP-Violin, our physically-informed version of it. Both are trained on three datasets, and each model is played on the same excerpts, so that every row of a table is the same 20 s of music under each model.

The models

DDSP (original)

The solo-instrument autoencoder of the DDSP paper (magenta/ddsp 3.7.0, solo_instrument.gin): a decoder (three-layer MLPs on the pitch and the loudness, a 512-unit GRU, a 512-unit MLP) outputs, 250 times a second, an amplitude, the distribution over 60 harmonics and 65 noise-band magnitudes; a harmonic oscillator bank and a filtered noise source are summed and passed through a trainable 3 s reverb that stands for the body and the room together. Trained on the multi-scale spectral loss (L1 on the magnitude and on the log magnitude at six FFT sizes from 64 to 2048) with Adam at 3e-4 decaying by 0.98 every 10K steps, batch 32, 50K steps, as the published solo-violin checkpoint was.

DDSP-Violin (ours)

The same decoder and synthesis, with the harmonic source constrained by bowed-string acoustics: the harmonic levels follow a brightness tilt (one parameter, measured on the recording and given as a third input), the bow-position mask (notches at the harmonics of the bowing point; here the mask of the open position), and per-harmonic residuals limited to ±12 dB. The sum of harmonics and noise goes through a learned body filter (a 2048-tap impulse response); the + reverb variant adds the same trainable 3 s reverb as the original model. Trained with the same loss, optimiser, batch size and number of steps as the original.

One parameter extraction

Both models receive 16 kHz audio cut into 4 s examples every second, the pitch from CREPE (the full model with Viterbi decoding; untracked frames filled from the nearest tracked one), and the A-weighted loudness over 512-sample windows, 250 frames per second. The DDSP rows of this first version were trained with the library's own feature extraction (the same CREPE and loudness, read by its own writer); the runs on the shared extraction are in progress and will replace them.
